About

I am pursuing a Master’s degree in Computer Science at the University of Cincinnati, where my research explores how principles of biological intelligence can inform autonomous robotic systems. My thesis integrates biologically inspired spatial representations, associative memory, and vision-language models to enable embodied agents to understand and navigate their environments.

Alongside my academic research, I serve as a Graduate Research Assistant at the P&G Digital Accelerator @ UC, developing machine learning, data analysis, and robotic automation solutions. Prior to graduate school, I spent over three years in software engineering, developing scalable systems and leading cross-functional engineering initiatives.

My long-term interests lie in advancing intelligent robotic systems that can operate as capable partners to humans, reducing the need for repetitive tasks and enabling more opportunities for creative, social, and intellectual fulfillment.

Active Research

Neuroscience-Inspired Embodied Intelligence

Master’s Thesis · Advisor: Dr. Ali Minai

Investigating how biologically inspired neural architectures can enable embodied agents to learn spatial layouts, associate semantic information with physical locations, and execute language-guided navigation tasks.

My approach integrates hippocampal place cells, boundary vector cells, associative memory networks, and vision-language models to learn and retrieve spatial and semantic information through environmental exploration.

Embodied AI Place Cells Boundary Vector Cells Associative Memory VLMs Spatial Cognition Webots

Information-Theoretic Reasoning in Large Language Models

Research Collaboration · Dr. Tianyu Jiang

Investigating the ability of large language models to ask informative questions, reason under uncertainty, and acquire information efficiently in open-domain interactive settings.

My work involves developing Bayesian belief-tracking frameworks to quantify information gain and studying how language models balance information acquisition against the costs of asking questions.

LLMs Bayesian Inference Information Gain Uncertainty Interactive Reasoning Cost-Aware Reasoning
